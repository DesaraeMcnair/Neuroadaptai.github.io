Founder & Researcher Story

The inspiration behind Neuro Adapt AI

The idea for Neuro Adapt AI began with my daughter.

When she first received her AAC (Augmentative and Alternative Communication) device, I felt hopeful. Technology was providing a new pathway for communication and a chance for greater independence.

At the same time, I encountered a challenge shared by many families introducing assistive technology into their lives.

Before the device, my daughter and I had developed our own communication system built through connection, observation, and trust. She communicated through gestures, behaviors, routines, and patterns that I learned to recognize over time.

When the AAC device was introduced, I found myself asking a question many caregivers face:

How do I teach my child to communicate through technology when I am still discovering everything she understands and wants to express?

AAC systems provide powerful tools through symbols, pictures, and language. However, these systems often depend on caregivers and professionals anticipating what options a user may need.

What happens when we do not yet know everything a person is capable of expressing?

As I watched my daughter interact with technology, larger questions emerged.

What if technology could learn her the same way I was learning her?

What if technology could recognize patterns in communication, preferences, routines, and behaviors?

What if technology could evolve alongside the individual instead of requiring the individual to adapt to the technology?

That question became the foundation of Neuro Adapt AI.

Today, Neuro Adapt AI represents a research vision centered on adaptive assistive intelligence.

Rather than functioning as a static communication tool, Neuro Adapt AI explores how artificial intelligence, human-computer interaction, and assistive technology can work together to create systems that continuously learn, personalize support, and help individuals communicate, learn, and navigate the world in ways that reflect their unique needs and strengths.

As a researcher, computer scientist, educator, and parent, my mission is to advance inclusive artificial intelligence that empowers neurodivergent individuals, individuals with disabilities, and their families through adaptive, human-centered technology.

Neuro Adapt AI began as a question inspired by my daughter.

It continues today as a commitment to ensuring that technology learns from people, rather than expecting people to learn around technology.

The future is not humans adapting to technology.
The future is technology adapting to humanity.
